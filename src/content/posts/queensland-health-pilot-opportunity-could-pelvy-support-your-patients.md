Calling Queensland Health pelvic health, pelvic pain, urogynaecology, gynaecology, obstetric, colorectal, urology, continence and nurse-led pelvic health services.

Hi, I’m Amelia Godfrey, a pelvic health physiotherapist and founder of Pelvy.

Pelvy is a clinician platform and patient app that helps improve adherence to exercises, medications, education and other health advice between appointments. We also provide evidence-based pelvic health education for people who are waiting to access care.

Since launching in December, Pelvy is now used in 112 clinics across 6 countries and has supported more than 5,300 patients.

I’m currently applying for the Queensland Health Innovation Challenge, which funds pilot projects within Queensland Government health services that improve patient outcomes and reduce pressure on the public health system.

## The Opportunity

The Innovation Challenge provides grants of up to $200,000 to support pilot projects within Queensland Health.

Importantly, this funding is designed to support running the pilot, not just purchasing software.

Funding could be used for:

- Project coordination
- Implementation
- Evaluation and data collection
- Development of new Pelvy features requested by participating clinicians or services

There would be no cost to participating Queensland Health services or patients.

## What is Pelvy?

Pelvy allows clinicians to prescribe:

- Education
- Exercise programs
- Videos
- Treatment advice
- Medication reminders
- Follow-up recommendations

…all in one place.

Clinicians manage programs from their computer while patients access everything through the Pelvy app.

This helps to:

- Improve patient adherence
- Reduce paper handouts and multiple follow-up emails
- Reduce clinician administration
- Support patients between appointments

## How could Queensland Health use Pelvy?

### 1. Support patients already receiving care

Pelvy can support patients attending:

- Pelvic health physiotherapy
- Pelvic pain services
- Urogynaecology
- Gynaecology
- Obstetrics
- Midwifery
- Continence services
- Colorectal services
- Urology

Members of the multidisciplinary team can prescribe education, exercises and reminders in one place, helping patients better manage their care between appointments.

Examples include:

- Pelvic floor exercise programs
- Vaginal oestrogen reminders
- Pessary education
- Medication information
- Postpartum recovery advice
- Surgical preparation information

### 2. Support patients while they’re waiting

One of the biggest opportunities is supporting patients on waiting lists.

Services could provide education before patients are seen, helping them better understand:

- Their condition
- The role of pelvic health physiotherapy
- Self-management strategies
- Available treatment options
- When to seek earlier care

Services can use existing Pelvy resources, upload their own resources, or create videos specific to their service.

## Examples

### Pelvic Pain Services

Patients could access education about:

- Different pain mechanisms
- Medication options
- The role of physiotherapy and other allied health
- PPFA resources
- QENDO symptom tracking
- Endo33 referral pathways

Specialists could also create short educational videos about medication options or common questions that patients could review before their appointment.

### Stress Urinary Incontinence

- Pelvic floor exercise programs
- Urethral sphincter training
- Vaginal oestrogen
- Pessary education
- Earlier referral to pelvic health physiotherapy

### Pelvic Organ Prolapse

- Pelvic floor exercises
- Vaginal oestrogen
- Pessary information
- Conservative management strategies

### Obstetric & Postpartum Services

Midwives, physiotherapists and obstetricians could provide education while patients wait for appointments or to reinforce advice following discharge.

### Colorectal Services

Many colorectal patients receive similar education during their first appointment.

Pelvy could provide education on topics such as:

- Bowel positioning
- Defecation techniques
- Constipation management
- Laxative education
- The role of pelvic health physiotherapy

This allows patients to begin learning before they attend clinic while reducing repeated education for clinicians.

## This approach has already shown promise

While working in the Darwin Endometriosis and Pelvic Pain Clinic, we provided patients with education before they entered the service.

Many patients:

- Required fewer appointments
- Accessed physiotherapy earlier
- Were able to discuss medication options with their GP using PPFA resources before seeing the specialist
- Better understood the role of different members of the multidisciplinary team

Some patients ultimately didn’t require an initial specialist appointment once they understood that physiotherapy was the most appropriate first step.

## No Cost. No Integration. Minimal Administration.

A key advantage of this pilot is that:

- There is no cost to participating services
- There is no cost to patients
- Pelvy does not require integration with existing clinical software
- Implementation and coordination would be supported through the grant
- The aim is to create minimal administrative burden for participating services
